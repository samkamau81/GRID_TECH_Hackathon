Appendix

The evidence behind the recommendation

How Peak Power turns a customer's answers into a plan, which data it stands on, what that data does and does not show, and what is still missing. Everything here is read from the code and files the Prototype ships with. Where something is not modelled, we say so.

How an answer becomes a recommendation

  1. Customer answersRevenue model, rent or price, carbon limit, full-campus deadline, grid-connection delay, land for onsite power, weather year, grid stress.
  2. Plan library384 candidate power plans, each already simulated hour by hour and replayed through the packet's official checker.
  3. Re-simulationWhen a setting differs from the packet case, onsite gas is re-sized and the plan is re-run for all 87,600 hours (10 years).
  4. Economics10-year revenue minus power cost, with unserved energy priced at $1,000 per MWh.
  5. RecommendationThe opening month with the best profit among plans that fit the land, meet the carbon limit, serve the load and meet the deadline.

The model is a deterministic simulation and a rule-based screen. There is no statistical or machine-learning model, no fitted weights and no forecasting in this application.

Scoring and weighting

Overall grade

The Overall grade combines the scored dimensions with a weighted average of grade values (Good = 0, Moderate = 1, Bad = 2). Weights: Energy 2, Policy 1.5, every other scored dimension 1. "Not graded" dimensions are left out of the average, not counted as good or bad.

  • Bad if Energy is Bad, or the weighted average is 1 or more.
  • Excellent if no dimension is Bad and the average is 0.35 or less.
  • Good if at most one dimension is Bad and the average is 0.6 or less.
  • Moderate otherwise.

Special cases

  • Outside Texas Energy is not graded, so the grid queue wait (Time to power) stands in for it with weight 2.
  • If the energy stand-in has no grade (for example ISO-NE, with no measurable queue), Overall is Bad when two or more scored dimensions are Bad and Not graded otherwise.
  • Project cards add Land and Grid reliability. A project whose 10-year plan fails the reliability test is graded Bad overall.
  • The grid delay for a project changes only the Energy row.

The weights are judgement calls, not estimates fitted to outcomes. They are visible in the code and are the first thing to challenge with a customer.

Energy, grid and finance calculations

CalculationAs implemented
DemandPacket 75 MW campus, one reference year of 8,760 hours repeated for 10 years (87,600 steps). After opening, load starts at one third of full and ramps: f = min(1, 1/3 + k/15), k = months since opening, so full demand arrives 10 months after the first service month. Optional growth after full campus is compounded monthly.
Grid import limit25 MW from month 18, 50 MW from 24, 75 MW from 30, each shifted by the grid-connection delay. Stress events cut imports (60% in the packet case; 40% in the tighter case; optional 2-week August heat wave at 50%).
DispatchHour by hour. Contracted wind, solar and nuclear energy is used first (it arrives over the same grid connection, so it shares the import limit). During a grid-stress event, movable load can be relocated and batch load can be paused, by the plan's policy. The battery covers shortfalls. The rest comes from onsite gas and merchant grid purchases: in cost mode, cheapest first by hourly price; in carbon mode, grid first and gas in order of lowest emissions. Load still unserved is recorded and charged.
Battery (BESS)4-hour battery, round-trip efficiency 0.85, up to 365 cycles a year. Charges below $45/MWh, discharges above $100/MWh. It may charge from any source behind the meter; discharge earns clean credit only in proportion to the clean share of that year's charging.
Power costPer resource: fixed charge (CAPEX annualised with a capital recovery factor at 8% over the resource's life, plus fixed O&M) + energy payments (contract $/MWh on available output; gas $/MWh plus heat rate × gas price) + merchant grid purchases at the hourly price. Relocated load costs $123.5 per MWh (1.05 × $70 + $50) and interrupted work $1,000 per MWh; these two are shown separately from the plan's energy cost. Unserved energy is penalised at $1,000 per MWh in the profit calculation.
Carbonkg CO₂ per MWh = onsite gas emissions + grid purchases × grid emission factor (0.3329 t/MWh in year 1, declining 3% a year), divided by energy delivered. The carbon limit is tested on the 10-year average, or on every year if "stricter" is selected.
Revenue modelsLease: rent per kW-month by load tier × MWh served. Pass-through: rent + power cost × (1 + markup). Bundled: a price per MWh delivered. Hybrid: tier-based rent for the always-on and movable tiers, cost plus markup for the movable tier, and a price per MWh for the batch tier. Profit = revenue − power cost − unserved penalty.

Portfolio comparison logic

  • Each project is a site with its own grid delay, acres, permit assumption and stress case. State is kept per project, so switching projects does not carry settings across.
  • For every possible opening month the app picks the plan with the best 10-year profit that fits the land, meets the carbon limit and the wind-and-solar share cap, and serves the load.
  • The recommendation is the feasible opening month with the highest profit within the deadline. Neighbouring months are shown beside it so the trade (earlier revenue against more gas cost) is visible.
  • The alternatives table shows the recommended plan next to a cleaner plan (same opening month, lowest carbon that passes), a faster plan (earliest credible opening) and, when a carbon limit is set, a cheaper plan without that limit. It states the dollars per tonne of CO₂ the limit costs.
  • Relocation and interruption costs are reported separately from the business assumptions (rent, markup, price).

Assumptions and limitations of the model

  • One weather and price year (ERCOT 2023, from the packet) is repeated for ten years; the "weather year" setting scales wind and solar, it does not load other years.
  • Only ERCOT has an hourly model. Counties elsewhere are screened with the grade rules above, not simulated.
  • The 384-plan library is a coarse search, not an optimiser; re-sizing under changed settings adds onsite gas only.
  • Costs are screening estimates, not quotes. Business assumptions (rent tiers, markup, bundled price) are team inputs, not market data.
  • Grades are ordinal bands with cut-offs chosen by the team; a county just below a cut-off is not meaningfully different from one just above.

    Read this first

    These are exploratory correlations in the data the application ships. They describe how the variables move together across counties or states. They do not show that one thing causes another, they are not predictions, and they are not used by the model. Nearby counties share geography and grid regions, so observations are not independent and the confidence ranges are optimistic. The statistic is Spearman rank correlation (ρ), which measures whether two variables rise together without assuming a straight line. ρ near 0 means no monotonic relationship.

    3. Do the model's grades add independent information?

    Analyses we could not perform, and why

      Technical limitations

      Data availability

      • No electricity price, outage (SAIDI/SAIFI) or substation-capacity data outside Texas.
      • Policy is graded by state only; no county or city zoning data.
      • Climate is not stored. Cooling load is not modelled by location: the campus load profile is the packet's.
      • Water and hazard values are missing for a small number of counties (shown as Not graded).

      Geographic resolution

      • County centroids stand in for sites; a county can contain very different land.
      • Time to power is regional, not local. Eight regions carry all the information.
      • Fiber uses facility locations, not routes; unregistered facilities are absent.
      • County outlines are generalised to about 4.7 km.

      Modelling assumptions

      • One historical year repeated; no multi-year weather or price risk.
      • Grade cut-offs and weights are expert judgement, not calibrated.
      • Business inputs (rent, markup, price) are assumptions.
      • The plan library is a pre-simulated search space, not a global optimum.

      Validation still required

      • Compare grades with outcomes at real sites (queue-to-energisation times, utility load studies).
      • Sensitivity analysis of the grade weights and cut-offs.
      • Independent review of state policy readings; they change monthly.
      • Back-test the plan economics against a second price year.

Data sources
  1. Energy. Hourly dispatch model, Texas (ERCOT) only, built from the reference dataset. Outside Texas the Overall grade uses the regional queue wait until hourly prices for those regions are added.
  2. Time to power. Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory. Queued Up, data through 2025. Median years from interconnection request to operation for plants online in 2021 to 2025, by grid operator area. It tracks power plants, not data center loads.
  3. Wind and solar cap. U.S. Energy Information Administration. Hourly Electric Grid Monitor, 2025 net generation by grid operator, via Catalyst Cooperative's PUDL.
  4. Location. U.S. Census Bureau county boundaries, via us-atlas. Distances to 57 metro anchors are computed here.
  5. Fiber. CAIDA. IXP Dataset, built on PeeringDB, Oct 2026 release. Facility locations only, not fiber routes.
  6. Policy. State actions found in public sources, Nov 2026: ElectricChoice moratorium tracker; ArentFox Schiff on state regulation; Venable on tax incentives; NCSL on state incentives. Verify before relying on any state.
  7. Water. World Resources Institute. Aqueduct 4.0, baseline water stress, matched to counties.
  8. Natural hazard. Federal Emergency Management Agency. National Risk Index, v1.20, Dec 2025. Expected annual building loss per dollar of buildings.
  9. Carbon reference. Ember, 2025, via Green Calculus. The US grid averages about 384 kg CO₂e per MWh. There is no industry standard cap, so the caps here are examples. Tenants usually set clean energy goals instead.
  10. Land. Acres are entered for each project. They are checked against our equipment allowances per MW: 0.05 acres for engines and fuel cells, 0.1 for turbines, 0.15 for a 4 hour battery. For scale, the U.S. EPA Catalog of CHP Technologies (Sept 2017) lists equipment power density of 35 to 50 kW per square meter for reciprocating engines, 20 to 500 for gas turbines and 5 to 20 for fuel cells. Our allowances sit well above bare equipment, leaving room for access, fuel handling and growth. The battery allowance and the acre cutoffs are our own assumptions.
  11. Grid reliability. Our dispatch model. The stress case is a design assumption set per project: 7 events a year of 3 to 7 hours with grid imports cut to 60% of normal (40% at tight sites), plus a 5 day January wind and solar drought. It is not fitted to a dataset. It is motivated by real events: the FERC, NERC and Regional Entity staff inquiry into Winter Storm Elliott (report released Sept 2023), when nearly 90,000 MW of generation was out at the same time, and the NERC incident review Considering Simultaneous Voltage-Sensitive Load Reductions (Jan 2025), where about 1,500 MW of data center load moved to backup power at once in July 2024.

Grade cutoffs are our own, applied to these sources.

2Reliability · how far the plan can be pushed

Why we cap wind and solar

A plan that gets 80% or 90% of its energy from wind and solar looks cheap on paper, but real systems do not run that way without very large storage or firm backup. For scale, EIA hourly data for 2025 shows ERCOT, SPP and CAISO each got 37% of their energy from wind and solar. Among the 31 US grid operators above 15 TWh, the highest was Colorado's Xcel area at 45%, and the only one above that was a small New Mexico utility (PNM, 71%), which leans on its neighbors. Plans above the cap are removed before anything is ranked, and a plan within the cap still has to ride through a 5 day drought and the site's own grid stress case without leaving load unserved. The stress case can be made harsher but not softer than the site's own.

Data and model. Power costs, carbon, demand and its tiers, wind and solar profiles, prices, grid schedule, stress events and the seven resource offers come from the symposium packet (v6.0.1, V2: the battery may charge from the grid, up to 53 MW); builds start from 384 pre-simulated plans, each replayed through its official checker, and are re-run hour by hour in your browser with the checker's accounting. Revenue models, demand growth, weather years, contract delays, forecast events and the rented generator ($350/MWh, 0.74 t CO₂/MWh) are ours and labeled. The packet does not allow selling power back to the grid, and the battery never exports. Cost is the packet's decision cost (energy, equipment, contracts, $123.50/MWh to move work, $1,000/MWh for paused batch work), constant 2023 dollars, undiscounted. Dispatch follows simple rules, not an optimizer.

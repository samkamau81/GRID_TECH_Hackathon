About the project

What Is Peak Power?

Peak Power is a planning and operations simulator for a fictional data-center campus in Williamson County, Texas. It runs entirely in your browser: you choose how the campus is powered, and Peak Power replays every hour of the plan to show what it costs, how clean it is, and how it holds up.

The problem

A data-center developer has to balance speed to power, cost and environmental performance. Energy resources differ in when they become available, how they operate and what they cost, and the campus grid connection arrives in stages, which limits how much power can flow in at each date.

The objectives

  • Recommend a feasible mix of power sources that serves the campus.
  • Protect essential demand and account for flexible work during grid stress.
  • Explain the trade-offs behind every recommendation.

How the prototype shows it

The prototype walks through three steps, from the first decision to the live operation of the plant.

  1. 1

    Site Planning

    Compare power options and revenue models, set the limits that matter, and pick a start month.

  2. 2

    Run the plant

    Review the plant like a board: cost, alerts, contracts and the big decisions.

  3. 3

    Live Operations

    Watch the site run with a forecast, alerts and equipment status, and simulate events such as a wind lull or an outage.

What to expect

Pick a scenario, watch a plan take shape, and test it against stress. Peak Power is a screening tool built from benchmark assumptions: results compare options and are not a real project quote or a guarantee of uptime.

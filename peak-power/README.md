# Peak Power

A data-center development tool for a 75 MW campus: an introduction page, a Prototype (a portfolio of projects, a US county site finder,
energy and finance planning, board review and live operations), and a Data Center Climate Explorer (a map of data centers worldwide
with current local weather).
It is a static site: plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, no build step and no framework.

| Page | File | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Introduction | `index.html` | Welcome page. Its **Next →** button opens the prototype. |
| Prototype | `prototype.html` | Four steps: Portfolio (projects, timeline, Find a site county map), Project plan (finance, energy), Run the plant, Live Operations. Runs entirely in the browser. Details: [`../docs/PROTOTYPE_INTEGRATION.md`](../docs/PROTOTYPE_INTEGRATION.md). |
| Data Center Climate Explorer | `data-centers.html` | Interactive map of data centers worldwide, with a United States view and current local weather from Open-Meteo. |

The app has exactly these three tabs, and all three link to each other. The Prototype was supplied as a Claude Artifact
(`Peak Power (Copy).html`, kept unchanged as the reference) and split into files by `../scripts/split_prototype_artifact.py`. The previous
prototype is in `archive/prototype-v1/`, and the original single-file build in [`../backup/PeakPower.original.html`](../backup/PeakPower.original.html).

## Run locally

The introduction and prototype also work when opened straight from disk. The explorer loads its data with
`fetch`, which browsers block for files opened from disk, so serve the folder:

    python -m http.server 8000      # from inside peak-power/

then visit http://localhost:8000 (introduction), `/prototype.html` or `/data-centers.html`.

From the repository root, `python data/Symposium_Packet/team_app/server.py` serves the same folder (and the
separate Python API). To share it publicly, serve only this folder, for example `ngrok http 8000` after the command
above.

## Weather (Open-Meteo)

Current conditions come from the Open-Meteo forecast API (`current=` temperature, feels-like, humidity, weather code,
wind, precipitation; Fahrenheit, mph, inches; `timezone=auto`) and are shown for a selected data center, a clicked map
point, or a searched place (Open-Meteo geocoding). Nothing is fetched on load or for every marker. The values are
modelled for the area, not measured at the site, and are current conditions only, not climate averages.

Reliability: responses are cached for 10 minutes (place searches 24 hours), identical in-flight requests are shared,
the client sends at most 30 requests a minute (the free API allows 600), an HTTP 429 pauses requests for a minute, and
every request times out after 10 seconds. If weather fails, the data center information stays visible with a retry button.

**Licence:** the free Open-Meteo API is for **non-commercial use only**; the data is CC BY 4.0, so keep the
"Weather data by Open-Meteo.com" attribution. Commercial deployment needs a paid plan, and a paid key must never be put
in browser code. There are no weather map overlays: Open-Meteo has no continuous weather tiles, and interpolating a few
point observations into a nationwide heatmap would not be valid, so weather is point-based.

## Site brief, county grades and power plants (Explorer)

Click the map, a data center or a search result and the **site brief** opens beside the map (a drawer over its right edge on mid-size screens,
under it on phones). It answers an investor's questions in this order:

1. **Verdict.** The Prototype's *Find a site* overall grade for the county, plus Time to power, Natural hazard, Policy and Cooling climate.
2. **What this means for a 75 MW plan.** The county's Time-to-power grade picks a grid-delay case (screening assumption: Good = on time, Moderate = 6
   months late, Bad = 12 months late; not graded = no case). The numbers are the **Prototype's own engine** for the packet case with that delay
   (`delayCase()` in `js/prototype.js`). Finding: a late grid mostly costs time and revenue (about 6 months of service and about $48M of 10-year profit per
   6 months late), not power price (under $0.50/MWh) or carbon (about +3 kg/MWh). Profit is labelled a business assumption. These are ERCOT (Texas) packet-case
   results: they show how much timing matters, not a county's electricity price or grid emissions, which are not modelled outside Texas.
3. **Power around here.** EIA-860 (2024) plants within 50 miles: operating capacity by fuel, zero-carbon share of nameplate, planned capacity and the five
   largest plants. Nameplate is supply, not headroom, and a wind or solar MW makes far fewer MWh than a gas MW, so read the share as grid carbon context only.
4. **Cooling climate.** Three full years of hourly temperature and humidity (Open-Meteo archive, ERA5): cooling degree days (base 65 F), 95th-percentile
   temperature, hours at 95 F or more, humid hours (wet-bulb at least 75 F, from Stull 2011), share of hours below 65 F, hours by cooling need and degree days by month.
   Graded Mild / Moderate / Demanding by `CLIMATE_RULES` in `js/climate-weather.js` (a team screening assumption, shown in the panel). Works worldwide.
5. **All eight Prototype factors** with a one-line reason each, and the weather now (fetched only when opened).

Two map overlays: **County grades** (3,106 contiguous-US counties coloured by any Prototype grade) and **Power plants** (14,518 EIA-860 plants, sized by MW, small
ones appear as you zoom). Their data loads only when first needed (about 0.1 MB of outlines and 0.2 MB of grades gzipped, plus 0.3 MB of plants).

**Data files and how to rebuild them**

| File | Built by | Source |
|---|---|---|
| `js/data/site-grades.js` | `node scripts/build_site_grades.mjs` (headless Chrome, about a minute) | Reads grades, facts and the delay cases from the Prototype's own code through a read-only hook (`window.PEAKPOWER_PROTOTYPE_API`), so the pages cannot disagree. |
| `data/processed/county-outlines.json` | `python -I scripts/build_county_outlines.py` | The Prototype's county map, converted back to latitude/longitude (it is a standard Albers layout; round trip checked). |
| `data/processed/eia860-plants.json` | `python -I scripts/build_eia860.py <zip>` (see the script header) | EIA-860 2024. |

Re-run `build_site_grades.mjs` after any change to the grading code or engine inputs; `tests/e2e/site-overlay.e2e.mjs` fails if the file is stale.

**Limits.** County outlines are generalised (about 5 km), so a point within a few km of a county line may land in the neighbour (the brief says when it is
near a line). Alaska and Hawaii have no county layer (their plants and climate still work). Climate is a model grid (about 10 km), not a site measurement, and
describes the climate, not a cooling design. Open-Meteo's free API is non-commercial only; keep the attribution.

## Data Center Climate Explorer (map and data)

**Data.** `data/data_centers.zip` holds an OpenStreetMap-derived extract: `data_centers.csv` (4,802 mapped
facilities, 32 columns), `country_summary.csv` and `reported_totals.csv` (figures from an independent tracker).
It has coordinates for every record, but no power-capacity field. Only about a third of records have a city, and
under half of the US records have a state (821 of 1,789).

**What the page shows.** Summary cards, top-five lists, and a map with Global and United States views, three layers
(clusters, individual markers, density heatmap), region, country, US state, city, facility-type and name/operator
filters, a legend, popups and a reset-view button. Everything is computed in the browser from the processed file,
and the statistics follow the filters.

**What it does not claim.**
- Counts are OpenStreetMap-mapped facilities, not a census; the page shows the tracker's higher reported total beside them.
- There is no power or capacity figure. "Mapped footprint" is building area where recorded.
- The heatmap shows the density of mapped locations, not electricity use or demand.
- 968 US records have no state in the source. Their state is derived from their own coordinates using US Census
  state boundaries, checked against the 821 records that do have one (99.9% agree), and marked "inferred" in
  popups. Cities are never inferred. Switch inference off with `--no-state-inference`.

### Refresh the map when the data changes

1. Replace `data/data_centers.zip` with the new archive (same three CSV files and columns).
2. From the repository root run:

       python scripts/process_data_centers.py

   It validates the records (invalid or missing coordinates and duplicate IDs are dropped and listed in the report),
   writes `peak-power/data/processed/data-centers.geojson` and `data-centers.report.json`, and prints a summary.
   It uses only the Python standard library; the first run downloads the US state boundaries into
   `scripts/.cache/` (needs internet), later runs reuse them. The output is identical for identical input.
3. Reload the page. Counts, lists and the notes at the bottom of the page update themselves.

## Dependencies and external services

- **Fonts:** Google Fonts (system fallbacks offline).
- **Explorer only:** Open-Meteo (weather and place search, called from the browser), Leaflet 1.9.4, Leaflet.markercluster 1.5.3 and leaflet.heat 0.2.0 from cdnjs (pinned, with
  integrity hashes), and OpenStreetMap map tiles. The page needs internet access for these and shows a message if
  the map library cannot load.
- **Prototype:** none; it makes no network requests of its own.

## Notes and limitations

- The prototype's `js/app/` files are classic scripts that share global scope and must load in the order given in
  `prototype.html`. They were moved out of the original single file, not reformatted, so their behaviour is unchanged.
- Individual map markers are drawn on a canvas and cannot be reached with the Tab key. Keyboard users can pan and
  zoom the map, and use the lists, filters and toggles, but cannot open an individual marker's popup.
- Several US cities share a name across states, and the city filter matches by name; combine it with the state
  filter when that matters.
- The creators' roles and biographies on the introduction page are placeholders; add photos by replacing the
  initials avatars (see the comment in `index.html`).
- The prototype's old entry URL (`/index.html`) now shows the introduction page; the prototype is at `/prototype.html`.
- Switching map layers within about a second of a zoom animation can log an error from markercluster's own timer; it is harmless and the map keeps working.
- Tests are in `../tests/` (see its README). There is no build tooling.
